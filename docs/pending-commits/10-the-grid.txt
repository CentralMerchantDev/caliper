The world is a grid of standard cells, and things dig into it

Mark: models take up standard blocks, with smaller measurements inside them --
quarters, eighths, sixteenths -- so each thing needs a set amount of space, a
road takes N cells, and it goes together like building blocks. And the model
carries the depth, so placing it makes the actual depth in the ground.

WHY A GRID AT ALL

Everything this project has got wrong, it got wrong in continuous metres. A
bench at 1.8 m and a lamp at 0.6 m, placed by two loops with different step
sizes, will eventually land 5 cm apart -- and nothing about that is detectable
without measuring every pair, which is exactly how 27 lamp posts came to be
standing inside benches. On a grid they either occupy the same cell or they do
not, and that is a question with an exact answer.

It also gives the asset lane something buildable to. "Make a straight road
piece" is vague. "Make a straight road piece one cell long" is a spec.

THE NUMBERS, AND WHY

  CELL   8 m    one road module. Long enough that a kilometre of street is 125
                pieces rather than a thousand, short enough to follow a
                coastline without visible faceting, and it divides the block
                figures already in WORLD-RULES -- a 120 m block is 15 cells
                exactly.
  DIVISIONS     full 8, half 4, quarter 2, eighth 1, sixteenth 0.5. Exact
                binary fractions on purpose: halves and quarters of 8 are
                representable exactly in floating point and thirds are not, and
                a grid whose steps do not land on the same numbers twice cannot
                be snapped to.
  LEVEL  4 m    one storey, exactly half a cell. "Twenty storeys instead of ten"
                is a change of one integer, not a re-model.

THE GRID IS ARITHMETIC, NOT AN ARRAY

At 8 m the 26 km world is 3,250 x 3,250 = 10.6 MILLION cells, and sixteen times
that if subdivisions were materialised. Nothing here stores a cell. Every
function is integer arithmetic on a coordinate, so the whole world is
addressable at no cost and only the cells something actually occupies are ever
recorded -- in the registry, which already held them.

THE WHOLE WORLD, WITH PARTS LOCKED

The grid covers all 26 km, not the built area. A locked region is PRESENT: it
has coordinates, terrain and a place in the world, and refuses placement with a
reason. Unopened land that simply did not exist would make the map end at the
edge of the town, and opening more of it later would move everything already
placed. "No open regions" and "no restriction" are deliberately kept as
different states, because they are opposites and one is easy to produce by
accident.

THINGS DIG IN

A model now carries `depth`, and placing it reserves the hole as well as the
part that shows. Reserving only what is visible is how two basements end up
sharing one hole while both look correct from above. It is also what makes the
strata load-bearing rather than decorative: a basement is dug through topsoil
and subsoil into clay, and the world can now say so.

THREE ARITHMETIC RULES THAT LOOK LIKE PEDANTRY AND ARE NOT

  cellOf FLOORS, it does not truncate. Math.trunc puts -1 and +1 in the same
  cell, and the world extends both ways from the origin -- the classic
  off-by-one that only appears west and north of centre.

  snap ROUNDS, it does not truncate. Truncating biases every placement in the
  world half a step south-west, which over 26 km is a systematic drift nobody
  notices until two things built to touch do not.

  cellsFor CEILINGS. A 9 m thing does not fit in one 8 m cell, and rounding down
  is the airport-apron bug in a single function: a thing declared to fit the
  ground that was checked, then overhanging it.

VERIFIED BY MUTATION

  snap truncates instead of rounding      -> the snapping test fails
  cellsFor floors instead of ceilings     -> the sizing test fails
  cellOf truncates toward zero            -> 2 addressing tests fail
  an empty open list unlocks the world    -> 3 tests fail
  depth ignored, nothing digs in          -> the basement test fails

Two of those had to be rerun. The first attempt at the last two used sed and the
patterns did not apply, so the suite stayed green -- which is an INCONCLUSIVE
result and was recorded as one, not as a pass. Redone with an exact-match
replacement that asserts it applied, both fail as they should.
