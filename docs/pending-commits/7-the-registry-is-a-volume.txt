The registry held heights it never read, and could not tell two questions apart

Two defects, both invisible on a green suite, because both answered a different
question plausibly instead of answering the right one wrongly.

ONE: HEIGHT WAS DISCARDED, AND THE DATA WAS ALREADY THERE.

reserve() has taken yMin/yMax since it was written, and bridges are registered
with real deck clearances -- arch 2-30 m, cable-stayed 2-55 m -- for the express
purpose of leaving the water beneath them navigable. overlapsReserved then threw
that away and answered in plan only. So a bridge blocked the channel it spans,
and a lamp head at 9.1 m blocked the pavement beneath it.

Nothing was missing. The height was recorded, carried, and then not read, which
is the kind of defect that survives review: every line involved looks correct on
its own.

It also made a comment in ground.js untrue the moment it was written. canPlace
passed { yMin, yMax } and described its occupancy check as volumetric; the
registry silently ignored the sixth argument. A claim in a comment is a claim.

Reservations that do not care about height still default to -Infinity..Infinity
and are unaffected, so every existing caller behaves exactly as before. Touching
is not overlapping, the same rule the plan axes already use: a deck resting
exactly on a datum is not inside it.

TWO: "MAY I BUILD A HOUSE HERE" AND "MAY I STAND HERE" GOT ONE ANSWER.

Roads are reserved across their FULL right of way, footway included -- correctly,
because a plot may not be laid over a pavement. But it means every lamp, bench,
bin and parked car in the world is INSIDE a road's rectangle by construction.
Asked without a filter, the registry refuses all of them and empties the street
scene, and it looks like placement stopped working rather than like the wrong
question was asked. That is not hypothetical: it is why the props commit decided
placement prop-against-prop in the renderer instead of asking the registry, and
the note there said this filter was what was missing.

overlapsReserved and findFree now take ignoreKinds / onlyKinds. The land's
canPlace ignores the kinds that DEFINE ground -- road, bridge, rail, waterway,
park, farm, plot -- because its surface step has already decided whether this
thing may stand on that kind of ground, and keeps the kinds that physically
OCCUPY it. The ignore list is derived from the kind-to-surface map rather than
typed out again: a kind that maps to a surface is, by definition, one that
defines ground.

findFree takes the same options and passes them through. A search for room that
asks a different question from the placement check will confidently return
somewhere that placement then refuses, and the caller cannot tell they disagreed.

VERIFIED BY MUTATION

  discard the height range again        -> 2 registry tests fail
  remove the kind filters               -> 3 registry tests fail
  canPlace stops passing ignoreKinds    -> 2 land tests fail
  canPlace skips the surface step       -> 2 land tests fail

THE THIRD ONE INITIALLY PASSED, AND THAT WAS THE MOST USEFUL RESULT HERE.

Removing ignoreKinds from canPlace changed nothing, because every test in
ground.test.ts built the land with NO registry -- so the occupancy branch never
ran, and the single most important integration in the file was uncovered while
the suite reported 12 green. The hole was not in the code; it was in the shape
of the tests, which is the harder kind to see.

Three tests now build the land WITH a registry and cover it end to end: a car
may stand on a carriageway a road defines; another car already parked there
refuses it by name; and a lamp is refused on a carriageway at the SURFACE step,
for the ground it is on rather than by colliding with something. That last one
is the rule stated plainly -- a lamp is not moved out of the road afterwards, it
was never allowed there.

One test in this commit was also wrong on its first writing: it asserted that a
4 m boat passes under a deck whose underside is at 2 m. The registry was right
and the expectation was nonsense, which is the more dangerous of the two --
"fixing" the code to satisfy it would have opened every bridge in the world.
The corrected version is annotated with why, because the next person to read it
will have the same instinct.
