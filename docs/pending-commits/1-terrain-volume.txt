The land was a mask over nothing, and cliffs were the grass colour darkened

Mark's diagnosis was exact: "you have it as a map over nothing but it needs
depth and a core just like the earth has." Three separate things made that true.

The outer mesh -- most of the modelled world by area -- was built with
skirtDepth 0, the argument that means "emit no side wall at all". So beyond the
detail tile the world had no underside: a surface with an open edge and nothing
behind it. It now goes to bedrock like everything else.

Where a skirt did exist it was two vertices per column, top and bottom, coloured
by darkening the surface colour. A two-vertex wall can only ever render a
gradient, so every cliff and cut was a smear of the grass above it -- which is
precisely why nothing read as having a material. It is now one vertex per
stratum boundary against a shared depth table, so a cut shows topsoil, subsoil,
clay and gravel, weathered rock and bedrock, and the bands stay horizontal while
the ground above them is not.

BEDROCK_Y is -175, chosen to meet the abyss plane rather than to look right, so
the base closes instead of hovering above it.

The first version of the skirt builder pushed only the stratum boundaries that
fit inside each column, and its failure is worth recording because it is the
kind this project exists to refuse. Measured on a 12-column sample from -174.5 m
to 2150 m, the per-column counts came out 2,3,6,6,6..., the index loop took its
stride from the first column as 2, and every index it generated stayed IN
BOUNDS. No crash, no error -- just a wall drawn from the wrong vertices, in
silence. Clamping each boundary to its own column's depth keeps the stride
constant and degenerates the quads that do not fit, and the stride is now
asserted where it is assumed rather than trusted.

Cost is ~10,300 vertices across both meshes, most of it the outer skirt that
previously did not exist.
