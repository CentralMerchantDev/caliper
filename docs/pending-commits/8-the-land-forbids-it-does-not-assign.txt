The land forbids; it does not assign

A correction to the model, made on Mark's reading of it, and the difference
matters more than it looks.

WHAT WAS WRONG

The first version treated a ground type as a permission: this square metre IS
carriageway, therefore only a car may be here. That reads as strict and is
actually the wrong shape, because it makes the land decide the city. Any dry
ground could become a road, a house, a park or nothing. The land has no opinion
about which -- and a world whose ground is pre-assigned cannot be built in by
anyone, which matters rather a lot for something that is going to have a build
mode.

WHAT IT IS NOW

The land only ever says NO, and only about what it genuinely knows: open water
will not carry something that stands on the ground, a cliff face carries nothing
with a footprint, a foreshore carries nothing permanent. Those are facts about
terrain, not zoning.

Everything else is decided by WHAT IS ALREADY THERE. A sidewalk is not a
permission the land granted; it is a thing somebody built, and having been built
it accepts people, lamps, hydrants and signs and refuses cars. Put a carriageway
on the same ground instead and it accepts cars and refuses lamps. Same square
metre, opposite answers. The rule travels with the object, not with the earth.

OPEN ground accepts ANYTHING, and that entry is the one that makes the world
buildable. If unbuilt land carried a permission list, every new kind of object
would need the land amended before it could be placed anywhere at all.

AND SIZE DECIDES

"A parcel too small will not take a road; a house-sized plot will not take a
tower." canPlace now measures the thing against the host it is going onto, and
refuses two different ways: `too-big` when it will not fit at all, `overhangs`
when it fits but not at this position.

That second one is not pedantry. It is the airport apron that overhung its own
vetted platform by 110 m with a whole row of aircraft standing beyond it --
fitting and being positioned are different questions, and answering only the
first is how that happened. Together with the stadium at -7.0 m in the water,
389 plots inside feature footprints, and 328 promoted arterials whose blocks
were set back for a narrower road than the one built, that is four
different-looking bugs and one question nobody asked: is there actually room.

canPlace is now five checks in order -- terrain, acceptance, size, fit, occupancy
-- and each refuses with its own reason and a number.

A REAL DEFECT FOUND ON THE WAY

reserve() has always looked like it accepted a `surface`, and never stored one.
whatIsAt did not return it either. So a caller could reserve a strip as SIDEWALK
and it would silently come back as CARRIAGEWAY through the kind fallback -- which
is exactly how "on the road" and "on the pavement" became the same fact in the
first place. Found because a test asserted a lamp belongs on a sidewalk and got
told a carriageway carries no lamps. Now stored and returned, which is also the
mechanism the road kit needs to decompose a right-of-way into strips.

VERIFIED BY MUTATION

  OPEN carries a permission list instead of anything  -> the buildability test fails
  skip the acceptance check                           -> sidewalk/carriageway fails
  skip the sizing check                               -> too-big and overhangs fail
  water refuses everything, bridges included          -> the bridge test fails

Every refusal is paired with a case that must succeed: a bench is refused on
water and a bridge crosses it; a lamp is refused on a carriageway and accepted
on a sidewalk; a tower is refused on a house lot and a house is accepted.
Without the pairs, a canPlace that always answered the same way would satisfy
half of these.

TWO OF MY OWN TESTS WERE WRONG FIRST

The sizing tests picked the first dry point in the search and got a mountainside,
where a 30 m tower is refused for the terrain under its corner rather than for
being too big for its plot. That is a pass for the wrong reason, which is worse
than a failure because it looks like evidence. They now search for ground that is
open and flat for 40 m around, so the test about size is answered by size.
