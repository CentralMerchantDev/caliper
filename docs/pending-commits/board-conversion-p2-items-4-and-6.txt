ITEM 6 (snap plot origins to whole metres): attempted, a real fix was
built and measured, and then REVERTED after finding a serious
regression -- reported here rather than shipped quietly.

Rounding generateBlocks()'s and generateSettlement()'s block bounds to
the metre (the actual root cause -- coordinates lost their whole-
metre-ness at WORLD_SCALE's float multiplication) reached the stated
goal exactly: 15,818 of 15,818 plots round-tripped cleanly through
atomOf/atomOrigin, 100%, up from 200 of 16,209.

But it broke something else, found by measuring rather than assumed
safe: city-wide placed buildings fell from 17,105 to roughly 9,700
(43%). Root cause, confirmed directly by counting rows per block, not
guessed at: a rounded block.depth crosses subdivideBlock()'s exact
two-row threshold (depth >= 2*cls.minD + 8) for most blocks that were
previously just above it -- 1,078 two-row / 184 one-row blocks before,
210 two-row / 976 one-row after. Block count and class-mix stayed
within noise (2,534 -> 2,533 blocks, per-class counts flat), so this
is specifically the second-row collapse, not a class-selection shift.

Three rounding strategies were tried and measured, not just the first
one that came to mind: independent per-edge rounding (the above
result), outward rounding (floor/ceil, tried specifically to stop
blocks from ever shrinking below a threshold) -- which was far WORSE,
3,246 total plots, because it makes ADJACENT blocks overlap instead --
and width-preserving rounding (round one edge, then the width, to
halve the theoretical worst-case error). All three produced
essentially the same placement collapse, which is the actual finding:
the problem is not which rounding method is used, it is that
block.depth sits very close to this exact threshold for a large
fraction of the world's blocks (a tuning coincidence in the
avEff/stEff density-scaling math P2.3's own comment already names),
making the world fragile to ANY perturbation of that size, not just
a badly-chosen one.

Reverted rather than shipped: a 43% drop in the city's own buildings
is not an acceptable trade for address correctness, and finding out
WHY block depths cluster at this threshold -- and fixing it without
changing the density tuning itself -- needs more time than this pass
has. public/city-plan.js, test/boardAdapter.test.ts,
src/citySummary.generated.ts and test/testCount.generated.json are
all confirmed byte-identical to HEAD (git diff --stat, empty) --
nothing shipped that was not fully verified working.
