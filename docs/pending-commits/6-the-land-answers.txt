The land, as something that can be asked

First piece of the rebuild. Not a city, not a layout, not one coordinate of
anything built -- the ground that models get laid onto, and the rules for what
may stand on it. docs/WORLD-RULES.md is the specification; public/ground.js is
its implementation.

WHY THE LAND HAS TO ANSWER

Every object in this world decided for itself where it could stand, inside the
same statement that drew it. A lamp went every 52 m down both sides of every
road because that loop said so; a bench went on the same footway because a
different loop said so; neither could see the other, because neither was asking
anything. Measured: 27 lamp posts standing inside bins and benches, one pair
5 cm apart. Before that, a stadium at -7.0 m in the water, 389 plots inside
feature footprints, and 328 promoted arterials with blocks set back for a
narrower road than the one built.

Every one of those is the same shape of defect. Placement and geometry were one
statement, so there was no moment at which anything could ask "is this ground
free?" -- and a question nobody can ask has no wrong answer, which is why none
of it ever looked broken.

WHAT IT PROVIDES

  surfaceAt(x, z, t)   the ground type: WATER BEACH ROCK CARRIAGEWAY SIDEWALK
                       VERGE PARKING TRACK PLOT PARK FARM OPEN
  columnAt(x, z, t)    one vertical line through the world: ground height,
                       slope, water depth, the solid below as real thicknesses
                       down to bedrock, and what occupies it
  canPlace(spec, x, z) may this stand here -- and if not, WHY NOT

A ROAD IS NOT ONE SURFACE, and that is the rule that answers "a lamp cannot
stand in the middle of a road". A boulevard is a carriageway with a verge, a
sidewalk and parking on each side; those are different ground with different
rules. A lamp declares it stands on SIDEWALK and VERGE, and a carriageway is
neither -- so it is not moved out of the road afterwards, it was never allowed
there. Treating a right-of-way as one slab is what made "on the road" and "on
the pavement" the same fact, and therefore uncheckable.

THE WORLD IS A VOLUME

Strata move here from city-render.js, where they were literals. The only thing
that knew what the ground was made of was the code drawing a picture of it, and
a foundation, a basement, a cutting and a tunnel are all dug into that. The
renderer is one consumer of it, not its owner.

Air is addressable too, in bands from pedestrian to sky, because a bridge deck
holds air at 2-55 m while the water beneath stays navigable, and a lamp head
occupies air a bench may stand under. A world that only reserves footprints can
express neither, and will either forbid the bench or allow a post through it.

"NO" IS NOT AN ANSWER

canPlace refuses with a reason and a number: which surface it hit and where, or
how far the ground moves across the footprint against what limit, or what is
already there. A caller that cannot tell "in a river" from "too steep" can do
nothing about either, and a refusal nobody can read gets deleted the first time
it is inconvenient.

The three checks are deliberately separate and ordered -- surface, then fit,
then occupancy. Conflating them is how objects end up inside each other. Asking
the occupancy question when you meant the surface one empties an entire street,
because a bench on a pavement is legitimately INSIDE a road's rectangle.

Footprints are sampled across their whole area, not at their centre, with the
step adapting to the object and the total capped -- and the cap is reported in
`samples` rather than hidden, because a check that silently coarsens is a check
that silently stops working.

VERIFIED BY MUTATION

  sample only the footprint's centre       -> 5 tests fail, incl. the one for it
  ignore standsOn                          -> the sea test fails
  ignore clearance                         -> the clearance test fails
  ignore rotation on both axes             -> the rotation test fails
  put STRATA out of depth order            -> 2 tests fail

The rotation mutation had to be redone. The first attempt disabled rotation on
one axis only, so the footprint still changed shape and the suite stayed green.
That is an INCONCLUSIVE result, not a pass, and recording it as a pass would
have been the exact failure this project exists to refuse.

Every refusal test is paired with a case that must SUCCEED, because a canPlace
that always answers ok:true satisfies any test that only asks whether it ran,
and one that always answers ok:false satisfies any test that only checks
refusals. Both are useless and both look fine on a green suite.

Nothing is placed by this and nothing is stripped yet. 500/500 node tests,
tsc --noEmit clean.
