The rate-limit key was spelled in three places and only one was updated

Found by running the Cloudflare Worker suite, which had not been running at all:
its Linux workerd binary was missing from this machine, so `npm test` reported
the Node half and refused the rest. Two of its twelve tests were failing.

THE PRODUCT DEFECT.

The per-visitor rate limit is stored at `ratelimit/<visitor>/<day>`, and that
string was written out by hand in three methods of SpendCounterLogic. When the
address was replaced with a salted hash for retention reasons, exactly one of
the three was updated -- claimRun, the enforcer. Both readers kept building the
key from the raw address, so they addressed a key nothing has ever written.
Neither threw. Neither logged. They returned plausible numbers:

  * runsUsed returned 0 for every visitor, always. That is the number
    /live-status quotes back to them, so the page said "2 of 2 runs left" right
    up until /change-run answered 429. That is the SECOND time this exact
    defect has shipped here -- the endpoint written specifically to stop the
    visitor being told something false was telling them something false again.

  * refundRun decremented a key that did not exist. A run refused for
    concurrency was therefore never actually given back, and the write PUT THE
    PLAINTEXT ADDRESS BACK INTO STORAGE -- reintroducing the retention that the
    hashing was added to remove.

Fixed structurally rather than three times over: rateLimitKey() is now the one
place the key is spelled. A key derived in three places is a defect waiting for
its fourth caller.

Verified by mutation, since a green suite is not evidence a control exists.
Reverting each site to the raw address independently:

  claimRun   -> per-IP count test fails
  refundRun  -> per-IP count test AND "refused for concurrency does not cost the
                visitor one of their runs" both fail
  runsUsed   -> per-IP count test fails

THE TWO TESTS.

Both were failing for reasons of their own, and both were asserting things they
could not reach.

"reports the per-IP count" drove /change-run and then read /live-status.
/change-run answers 200 with an SSE stream, fetch resolves when the HEADERS
arrive, and the run is claimed inside the body. Measured: with the body never
pulled, the claim had not landed 6.8 s later. It was asserting a side effect it
never waited for. It now drives the enforcer directly and asks over HTTP, which
crosses every join the defect lives in with nothing racing, and covers the
refund path too.

"refuses a CONCURRENT run" opened MAX_CONCURRENT_PIPELINE_RUNS runs from ONE
address. The concurrency cap is global; the daily cap is per address; and
DAILY_LIVE_RUNS_PER_IP is 2 against MAX_CONCURRENT_PIPELINE_RUNS of 5. One
address can hold at most 2 leases and can never reach 5, so the third request
was refused by the daily limiter -- the right refusal, the wrong limiter, for
the second time in that one test. It now takes leases directly until the
coordinator itself reports the pool full, then checks that a fresh address with
a clean daily allowance is refused for concurrency and nothing else.
