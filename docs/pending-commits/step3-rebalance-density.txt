World rebalance, step 3, part 1: the barrier island stops being the city,
the mainland stops having a second one, downtown gets more sky

WORLD-REBALANCE-BRIEF.md §3, against docs/audits/WORLD-DENSITY-FINDINGS.md's
measured starting point. Arithmetic, not opinion -- every figure below is
node scripts/measure-density.mjs, before and after this commit.

THE BARRIER ISLAND WAS THE CITY. barrierSettlements() declared its densest
stretch "Ocean City", class TOWER, core density 0.85 -- effectively
saturated, and named as a second downtown in both word and effect. Every
slice is now VILLA (shore housing) or RESORT (beachfront), never TOWER or
MIDRISE, at a fraction of its old density:

    barrier island share of all plots     56.0%  ->  8.0%

Getting there took two changes, not one. Lowering core/edge alone (the
existing per-settlement knobs) only reached 31%: the falloff formula's own
wobble term has a floor of 0.62, so even a near-zero core/edge still leaves
moderate density through most of a settlement's middle band, only crashing
near the true boundary. settlementDensity() gained a `scale` field --
multiplies the whole result, for a settlement that needs to be thin
EVERYWHERE, not just past its own core/edge radius. Every other settlement
is unaffected (scale defaults to 1); only the barrier island's slices set
it, between 0.12 and 0.15.

THE MAINLAND HAD ITS OWN SECOND SKYLINE. coastal-6/7 ("Harbour City") were
declared TOWER, core 0.7 -- and §3 asks for "suburbs... thinning outward",
naming no mainland tower core. Demoted to MIDRISE: still the mainland's one
employment/harbour hub, at suburban rather than downtown scale. The other
mainland strips (Fernwood, Marchmont, Westgate, Stonebridge, Ridgeway) had
their core/edge moderated up from what WORLD-DENSITY-FINDINGS.md measured as
"almost vacant" (coastal-4 at 8/km², coastal-9 at 7/km²).

THIS SURFACED A SECOND, DEEPER PROBLEM THAN §3 NAMED: applying any of the
above required first disabling generateWorld()'s zoneCharacter override,
which was silently re-deciding almost every settlement's class regardless of
what SETTLEMENTS/barrierSettlements declared -- measured earlier this pass,
21 of 26 non-downtown, non-barrier settlements (81%). This is the same
two-sources-of-truth failure WORLD-DENSITY-FINDINGS.md §3 diagnosed for
TOWER settlements specifically, at the scale of the whole rebalance: setting
the barrier island's declared class to VILLA changed nothing, because
zoneCharacter turned around and reclassified it back to TOWNHOUSE before a
single plot was measured. zoneCharacter still runs and zoningChanges still
records what it would have done (now with `applied: false` on every entry),
so the disagreement stays visible and reportable -- it is simply no longer
applied, because this pass's own declarations are Mark's explicit, current
zoning decision, not a generic heuristic's guess at one.

DOWNTOWN: TOWNHOUSE is no longer a majority. §3: "towers in the middle,
mid-rise shoulders, housing at the edge... this is the skyline, it has to
read from the air." DENSITY_AT's top two rungs (0.45 for MIDRISE, 0.63 for
TOWER) were high enough that most of downtown's own intensity field never
reached them, even though it correctly peaks at the CBD and falls off from
there -- the SHAPE of the falloff was already right, just where each class
kicked in on that shape was too conservative. Lowered to 0.36/0.50:

    downtown TOWNHOUSE share    48%  ->  49% of a changed total (627 plots, unchanged count)
    downtown TOWER share         6%  ->  11% (80 -> 142 plots)
    world-wide TOWER-class plots 81  ->  206

TOWNHOUSE's raw count did not move -- its own threshold band didn't change,
so this is not yet "housing pushed to the edge", it is "more of downtown's
upper range now reaches MIDRISE/TOWER". Whether that is enough of a skyline,
or needs to go further, is Mark's call -- see docs/audits/WORLD-REBALANCE.md
and the renders alongside it. Not settled here.

REGRESSIONS FOUND AND FIXED BEFORE THIS COMMIT, NOT AFTER: the first attempt
at wider barrier-island block spacing (av 220/st 170, reasoning "wider
blocks, sparser city") tripped test/cityWorld.test.ts's ITE 183 m
walkable-block ceiling and its own "spacing follows character" check --
119 blocks over the line. Fixed by using VILLA/RESORT's own
CHARACTER_SPACING values (180/140, 180/145) instead of an invented number,
and reaching the density target through `scale` rather than wider blocks.

ONE REGRESSION NAMED, NOT FIXED HERE: test/cityWorld.test.ts's "industry
clusters at a transport node" now fails (15 industrial plots, floor is 20) --
disabling the zoneCharacter override dropped total WAREHOUSE-class plots
below its old count, and zoneCharacter had apparently been leaning some
other settlement toward industrial character as a side effect of something
unrelated to this pass. Tried widening the port settlement's own bounds;
made no measurable difference (reverted, not shipped as a no-op edit) --
the real constraint is more likely how much of that frontage is actually
buildable against the real coastline, not the declared rectangle's size,
and that needs actual investigation rather than a second guess. Left red
and named, for the same reason the seed pins are: a real number, not silent.

How it is known to work: node scripts/measure-density.mjs, every figure
above reproduced from the command beside it. node test/run.mjs and
npx tsc --noEmit follow in the next commit, alongside the full before/after
render set and docs/audits/WORLD-REBALANCE.md -- this commit is the
