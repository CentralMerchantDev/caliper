Commit PLACEMENT-CONTRACT.md Part 0 -- the grid is 1 metre, grouped 4 and 16

Mark's decision, given directly in chat: the board's real addressing unit
is the 1 m ATOM, not the 8 m CELL. QUADRANT (4 m) and BLOCK (16 m) are
named groupings for indexing only and do not constrain placement -- a
building may span more than one block, refused only for lack of room or
wrong ground, never for crossing a boundary.

Two numbers doing different jobs, not one conflated with the other: the
ATOM (1 m) is the addressing unit; the MODULE (8 m) is the standard
increment for footprints and road widths, a deliberate design choice for
tiling, not a rounding the grid imposes.

Why this is right and 8 m was not: the 8 m cell forced every real-world
ROAD_STANDARDS dimension to round outward (an 18 m street reserving 24 m
of ground). At 1 m, an 18 m street is 18 atoms -- the standards keep their
real values and simply fit. It also closes findings P0 had to leave open:
rampMerge/rampDiverge/railSwitch each carry a connector socket at a
genuine lane-width or track-gauge offset that could not be forced onto an
8 m boundary without falsifying ROAD_STANDARDS itself.

The cost is measured, not assumed: 26 km at 1 m is 676 million atoms,
free because grid.js stores nothing (pure integer arithmetic on a
coordinate) and world-registry.js answers by rectangle, not by cell.

What changes: grid.js's CELL=8 stops being the atom and becomes a named
grouping alongside 4 and 16 (the module-size role roadkit.js's MODULE_M
already plays). P0.4's snapCellsOutward is reverted. Footprints come from
a standard size table (8 building sizes, 5 road-class widths) plus named
exceptions, not from rounding outward.

Committed as Mark wrote it -- no wording changed.
