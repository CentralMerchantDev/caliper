Size against available space, and what you would have to clear

Mark, stating the rule the layer exists to enforce:

  "Once the initial city is laid out, the only thing that dictates what can go
   on land is the size of the block compared to the space available. If the
   condo is 10 x 10 and the space is 6 x 6 you can't put the condo -- unless you
   clear more space for it."

The first clause the layer already enforced. The second it could not express,
and the second is the one a builder needs.

WHAT WAS WRONG

An occupancy refusal named the FIRST thing it hit and nothing else:

    "building shed-north is already here"

Which is true, useless, and slightly misleading -- it implies removing that one
shed would do. Two sheds 6 m apart leave a 6 x 6 hole; a 10 x 10 condo is
blocked by both, and a builder told about one of them clears it, tries again,
and is told about the other. Worse, the refusal said nothing about whether
either could be removed at all, so there was no way to tell "move that shed"
from "that is a hillside".

WHAT IT SAYS NOW

    10.0 x 10.0 m needed; building shed-north, building shed-south are in the
    way -- clear them and this fits

and carries `blockedBy: [{ id, kind, owner, clearable }]` so a caller can offer
the action rather than parse the sentence.

`clearable` is the honest part. Rock and open water are listed as blockers --
they genuinely are -- and are never offered as clearable, because they are not
things somebody put there. Telling a builder to clear a hillside is worse than
telling them no.

registry.allOverlapping() is overlapsReserved without the early return: same
filters, same terrain rules, same insertion order, it just does not stop at the
first match. overlapsReserved answers "is this free", which is the right
question for placement and the wrong one for someone who has just been told no.

AND THE SECOND CLAUSE IS ACTUALLY EXERCISED

The test does not stop at the refusal. It closes both sheds and places the condo
in the space they leave, because "clear them and this fits" is a claim and a
claim that is never tested is decoration.

VERIFIED BY MUTATION

  refusal names only the first blocker again  -> fails
  allOverlapping stops at the first match     -> fails
  rock is offered as clearable                -> fails (after the test was fixed)

THE THIRD ONE SURVIVED FIRST, AND FOR AN INSTRUCTIVE REASON

The rock test used the mountain peak, where the SURFACE is already rock -- so
the placement was refused at the terrain step, `blockedBy` was undefined, and
the assertion sat inside `if (blockedBy)` and never ran at all. A conditional
assertion is a test that can silently not execute, which is a new entry in a
familiar pattern: it is the fourth test this session that passed for a reason
other than the one stated.

Rewritten to bury a footprint 60 m under FLAT OPEN ground, so the terrain step
passes and rock arrives as an occupancy blocker -- the path actually being
tested. It now fails as it should.
