The grade the application never ran, and a sky that was only half swapped

Mark, on the deployed page: "it's still really hazy, and I don't know what it is
causing this. It's like you're in a foggy atmosphere. It's just fog. There's no
crispness or cleanness or colour, it's really bland."

Then, after the first theory failed and he sent a second screenshot at midday:
"I don't think that is it. I think it is the camera settings, as it seems like a
blur effect."

He was right both times, and I was wrong both times.

IT WAS NOT FOG AND IT WAS NOT BLUR. THE APPLICATION RENDERED UNGRADED.

city.html runs a colour grade as the last pass of its composer: saturation x1.30,
a contrast S-curve about mid grey, a warm/cool split that separates sunlit from
shaded faces, and a highlight roll-off above 0.86 whose own comment says "a blown
horizon is the first thing that reads as 'render' rather than 'place'".

index.html -- the application -- has a composer too. It carried one bloom pass at
strength 0.02 and NO GRADE AT ALL. So the page a visitor opens rendered with 30%
less saturation, no contrast curve, no warm/cool separation, and a horizon free
to clip to white. That is the wash, exactly as described.

AND THIS IS THE PART THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE FIX

scripts/shoot.mjs renders city.html. Every image used to judge how this world
looks came through that grade, and the product never did. The measuring
instrument was flattering the thing it measured, and it went unnoticed for a long
time because both pictures were of the same city.

That is the third time in one session that a harness diverged from the thing it
was supposed to be measuring:

  * the browser check stubs the renderer, so a module-scope throw in the real
    renderer left it green
  * index.html's animation loop never advanced headlessly, so a camera that had
    never been positioned made a raycast return the origin, which read as a
    broken pivot marker
  * shoot.mjs renders city.html, so neither the pivot marker nor the sky nor the
    grade could ever appear in a shot of it

Each was found by a different accident. The pattern is one thing: I was reading
the instrument, not the product.

The grade now lives in public/colour-grade.js and BOTH pages import it. Two
copies of a look is how a look drifts; one copy cannot.

THE SKY WAS HALF SWAPPED, AND THE OTHER HALF NEVER ARRIVED

_buildCityBase removes the village's sky mesh, starfield, cloud group, moon mesh
and moon light -- correctly, because they are sized for a village whose clouds
sit at y = 160-250 m and wrap at +/-850 m, among downtown towers that reach 220 m
in a world 26 km across. That is the "sea of free floating objects" Mark
described, and removing them was right.

Nothing put a city-sized replacement back. Meanwhile draw() went on positioning
the moon, setting the moon light's intensity, fading the starfield and drifting
the cloud clusters every single frame -- against objects that were no longer in
the scene. Every guard passed. Every update landed on an orphan.

public/sky.js builds the replacement: a starfield on a 46 km sphere that fades in
at night and rotates slowly, a moon opposite the sun so it rises as the sun sets
without a second clock, and clouds as two rotating domes at 2.6 km and 4.2 km
carrying a procedural, seamlessly tiling texture. Clouds are a LAYER, not
scenery; they cannot be flown into. Built in city-render.js beside the sun and
the fog -- not in the shell -- so shoot.mjs can photograph them.

Cost: three draw calls, 14 to 17, written down here rather than found later in a
frame graph.

Their first version was invisible. Not missing -- a probe confirmed two visible
domes in the scene -- just white at 5-13% alpha, tiled 3x3, times a material
opacity of 0.42, over a pale blue sky. Measured, then fixed.

THE TOOLBAR LIED ABOUT THE TIME OF DAY

#tod-day ships with class="active" aria-pressed="true" in the markup, so from the
first paint the interface claims the world is set to Day. _overrideHour was null,
meaning nothing was overridden and the hour free-ran on sim time. So the control
asserted a state the world had never been in, and drifted further from it every
minute the tab stayed open.

Mark's own screenshot caught it: the Day button lit, the clock reading 05:00, and
a city washed grey-green by a sun barely over the horizon. He read it as fog. It
was five in the morning. The default is now 12, matching what the interface says.

This was a real bug and it was NOT his bug -- his second screenshot, at 13:00,
correctly killed it as the explanation.

THE OCEAN-TILE BENCHES: NOT REPRODUCIBLE ON MAIN

Mark reported a park bench at the corner of every ocean square. There are exactly
two paths that can create a bench: the procedural furniture loop, which refuses
any ground below 1.2 m and so cannot reach water, and the live-world renderer,
which is confined to a single quarter at a fixed location. A world build was
probed for instances standing on ground below sea level: 284 of them, and the
largest group is the 81 marina boats, which belong there.

Not reproduced. Mark guessed it was already fixed by commits he has not deployed,
and nothing here contradicts that. Worth a second look once the deploy lands.
