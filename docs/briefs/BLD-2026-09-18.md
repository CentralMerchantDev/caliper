## 3. CAM-2 — MARK DESIGNED THIS, BUILD WHAT HE DESCRIBED

> *"Like an eye view — an expanded eye view, not exactly what you would see but
> more — that allows you to look up so you can see the building above you, just
> like in real life. Most of the time you're looking forward and you can only
> see what's in your eye line. As you spin around you can look up. And when you
> pan out, that's where you'll see the height."*

Eye level, forward by default, free to look up. Height revealed by pulling back
and up rather than by fitting a tower into one frame.

**A building that leaves the top of frame reads as taller than one that fits.**
Real city photographs almost never contain a whole tower — you frame the street
and the building runs off the top, and that is what makes it feel tall. Cropping
is the tool. That is precisely why `36` felt wrong.

**This also closes a design question rather than opening one.** Mark raised
height compression — squashing tall buildings so they fit — and then answered it
himself with this camera. Do not implement compression. `storeys` is what
`baseValue` and `unitQuality` read, so a tower that renders at 30 storeys and
scores at 100 would be the render-and-rule-disagreeing defect *by design*, and
this project has hit that class four times by accident already.

## 4. CAM-3 — THE HAZE, AND WHERE I THINK IT IS

Mark on `30-board-scene-pass-fix3.png`: *"the buildings are extremely hazy, the
details have been lost... the clarity has disappeared because of something
overlaid... a gauze."*

**Three retunes have adjusted fog density and colour. None of them touched it.
That is the evidence the lever is somewhere else.**

Reading `30` directly: the mid-rise's window bands are milky, the tower's edges
are soft — and the **ground** texture at the bottom of frame is sharp and grainy.
Objects over-hazed, ground under-blended. That is the opposite of how real
distance haze behaves and it points at the fog's **near plane** starting far too
close: geometry twenty metres away being blended toward fog colour as though it
were two hundred out.

The detail is not blurred, it is **washed** — contrast lost, not focus.

**That is a hypothesis from one image, not a measurement. Verify it before
changing anything.** If the near plane is not the cause, say so and report what
is — that finding is worth more than a fix aimed at the wrong lever, and GRD-1
already proved the value of measuring first when UV retiling turned out to be the
wrong one.
